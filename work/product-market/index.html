APPROACH

What I did

  • Assessed product-level sales growth, mix quality, gross margin and discount pressure.
  • Compared growth contribution with gross-profit contribution to identify low-quality growth.
  • Analyzed customer groups through sales share, discounts, collection time and commercial-risk indicators.
  • Designed a customer-grading framework, management KPIs and prioritized managerial actions.
KEY FINDINGS

What came out of the analysis

  1. Total unit sales increased 21.6%, but B2 and D generated 58.1% of net unit growth while contributing only 16.8% of estimated gross profit.
  2. Product C showed a 26% margin and 6% discount with a profit-efficiency indicator of 1.62, while unit sales declined 6.9%.
  3. Weighted receivables were about 146.8 days; private and government hospitals represented 65% of sales and roughly 74% of collection pressure.
  4. In the final business scenario, unit sales rose 18.4% while gross profit fell 23.6% and gross margin declined from 42% to 31%, highlighting the difference between volume growth and economic-quality growth.
